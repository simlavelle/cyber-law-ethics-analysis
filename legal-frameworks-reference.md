# Legal Frameworks Reference (for Security Practitioners)

Plain-language summaries — not legal advice.

## CFAA — Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (18 U.S.C. § 1030)
The primary U.S. anti-hacking statute. Prohibits accessing a computer **without
authorization** or **exceeding authorized access**. Relevant whenever someone uses
credentials or accounts they shouldn't, or reaches data beyond their permission scope.

## ECPA — Electronic Communications Privacy Act (18 U.S.C. § 2510+)
Governs interception of and access to electronic communications and stored data. Relevant to
unauthorized network scanning, sniffing, and accessing others' communications/systems.

## SOX — Sarbanes-Oxley Act
Applies to public companies' financial reporting. Requires internal controls over financial
data — driving separation of duties, access controls, and audit trails on financial systems.

## GDPR — General Data Protection Regulation
EU regulation on personal-data processing. Relevant when handling data of EU residents;
requires lawful basis, data minimization, breach notification, and strong safeguards.

## Standards vs. regulations
NIST and ISO publish **standards/guidance** (not laws). CFAA, ECPA, SOX, GDPR, and FISMA are
**regulations/laws** with legal force. Use standards to implement controls; cite regulations
to establish legal obligation.
